    About

    This is a live version of a class project (CS 129). AI-writing detectors judge text by what it says, which makes them unreliable and unfair to non-native English speakers. This one looks only at the process: the timing of your keystrokes, your pauses and your deletions.

    For the study, people typed about 50 words twice: once writing freely and once copying a Wikipedia passage, in random order. A logistic regression on three features of their keystroke logs (time spent paused, how often they went back to delete, and how much they deleted each time) tells the two apart of the time on held-out typing. People writing their own words pause to think and go back to fix things; people copying type in long, steady bursts. Most of its mistakes are calling rushed, careless writing copied.
